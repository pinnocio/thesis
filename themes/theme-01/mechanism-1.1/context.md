# Context — Theme 1, Mechanism 1.1

## 1. Project context

This is the findings chapter of an MA thesis in sociology. The research examines how different nationalism types morally frame key ethical dilemmas of the Israel-Hamas war, using a corpus of online op-ed articles from the period around the second hostage deal in January 2025. The analytical framework is Feinstein’s five-type nationalism typology, operationalized through reflexive thematic analysis and grounded theory.

The five types are:

1. Critical
2. Ultra-nationalist
3. Ethno-religious
4. Ethno-republican
5. Ethnocentric

The current work is on **Theme 1: Closing the Present as a Moral Decision Moment**.

Hebrew working title:

**תמה 1: סגירת ההווה כרגע הכרעה מוסרי**

Within this theme, the current mechanism is:

**1.1 The relation between moral referent and collective as a moral test of the collective**

Hebrew working title:

**1.1 יחס פרט–כלל כמבחן מוסרי של הכלל**

The current subsection is:

**1.1.1 יחס פרט–כלל כמדד למבחן המוסרי של הכלל**

This document is now a context file for **Mechanism 1.1 as a whole**, not for the Critical type only. It preserves the accepted decisions for the Critical type and adds the updated decisions for the Ultra-nationalist type.

---

## 2. Core claim of Mechanism 1.1

The convergence between the Critical and Ultra-nationalist types is not substantive agreement. Their political and moral conclusions are opposed. The resemblance is at the level of **interpretive logic**: both judge the morality of a collective through the relation between that collective and a moral reference object.

The comparison is not based on identical objects, identical collectives, or identical moral actions. The asymmetry between the types is part of the finding.

A simple heuristic remains useful but must not be treated as the full analysis:

| Type | Initial heuristic | Required correction |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | The collective is moral only if it does not abandon the moral referent. | Do not reduce the whole type to “abandonment”; specify which collective and which referent, and what relation produces the moral test. |
| Ultra-nationalist | The collective is moral only if it does not sacrifice the many for the few. | Do not reduce the type to a generic “many vs. few” calculation. The future victims embody the threatened national collective; the hostages produce immediate moral pressure but do not define the axis alone. |

The working claim for 1.1.1:

> A moral referent and a tested collective are brought into relation, and the collective’s moral standing is judged through what that relation reveals.

Accepted Hebrew formulation:

> בלב המנגנון עומד מהלך פרשני חוזר: מושא ייחוס מוסרי וכלל נבחן מובאים זה לצד זה, והכלל נשפט מוסרית לאור מה שמתגלה ביחס ביניהם.

This formulation applies to both the Critical and Ultra-nationalist types, but the referents, tested collectives, and failures differ across types.

## 3. 1.1.1 vs. 1.1.2

The distinction remains central.

| Section | Question | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **1.1.1** | What is the moral test? | Shows how the collective is morally judged through its relation to a moral referent. |
| **1.1.2** | What does the test do to the present? | Shows how the moral test converges into a demand for immediate, binary decision and removes a legitimate middle ground. |

1.1.1 must not fully develop:

* no legitimate middle ground;
* urgency;
* present-moment closure;
* deal versus continued war as an explicit binary demand;
* moral conviction;
* the imperative of immediate decision.

Those belong mainly to **1.1.2**.

A single transitional sentence at the end of 1.1.1 is enough.

For the Ultra-nationalist type specifically: in 1.1.1 it is permissible to mention **deal**, **release of Palestinian prisoners/militants**, **surrender**, **“at any price”**, **future blood**, **deterrence**, **Hamas survival**, and **campaigns of compassion** only insofar as they explain the relation between the tested collective and the threatened moral referent. Do not develop them as a full binary structure of “war not deal,” “no middle,” or “now.” That belongs to 1.1.2.

## 4. Core terminology and decisions

### 4.1 “טיפוס”, not “דפוס”

Use **טיפוס** because Feinstein’s framework is a typology of nationalism types. Do not replace it with דפוס as the primary term.

### 4.2 “מושא ייחוס מוסרי”

Preferred analytic term:

**מושא ייחוס מוסרי** — a person or distinguishable human group through whom the morality of a broader collective is examined.

“פרט” remains in the section title because the mechanism is titled “יחס פרט–כלל”, but in the body it should usually be replaced with the more precise term **מושא ייחוס מוסרי**.

The referent may be:

* hostages;
* soldiers;
* fallen soldiers;
* families of hostages;
* Palestinian women;
* Palestinian children;
* children in Gaza;
* Palestinian prisoners/detainees;
* civilians;
* innocent people;
* people in Gaza;
* future Israeli/Jewish victims, when specified as concrete human groups;
* future hostages;
* citizens, Jews, soldiers, the next generation, or “כולנו”, when they function as the threatened human collective and not as an abstract state entity.

Not every collective is automatically a moral referent.

Important cautions:

* A state is not a moral referent if it remains an abstract institution.
* A whole public or society is not automatically a moral referent; it must function as a concrete threatened or judged human group in the article.
* A feeling is not a moral referent.
* A campaign is not a moral referent.
* Security, deterrence, victory, honor, blood, sacrifice, existence, or survival are not moral referents as abstract values. They can appear in the relation or in the moral test.
* Soldiers are not always moral referents; sometimes they are agents of the collective.
* Palestinians, Gazans, civilians, children, prisoners, and detainees can function as moral referents, but each case must be checked in context.

### 4.3 “קיום המדינה”

General rule: **“the existence of the state” is not a moral referent** if it is an abstract institutional object.

Ultra-nationalist refinement: when “קיום המדינה” or “ביטחון המדינה” functions as shorthand for the continued existence and safety of the Israeli-Jewish people, citizens, soldiers, future hostages, or “כולנו”, it may be used analytically only after being translated into a human referent:

* אזרחי ישראל;
* יהודים;
* חיילים;
* חטופים עתידיים;
* הדור הבא;
* הקולקטיב הישראלי־יהודי;
* “כולנו”.

Do not leave “קיום המדינה” as the object column or the moral referent without this translation.

### 4.4 “יחס” = relation/configuration, not attitude

When used as the central analytic term, **יחס** means relation/configuration: how the tested collective and the moral referent are brought together, and what becomes visible about the collective through that relation.

Do not let the analytic noun **יחס** slide into the meaning of “way of treating” or “attitude.” Treatment verbs are allowed and often necessary — מפקיר, מסכן, מכיר, משתיק, מבזה, מנכיח — but they are evidence of the relation, not the definition of the relation itself.

Preferred body-text formulation:

**היחס שבין מושא הייחוס לבין הכלל הנבחן**

Use “יחס פרט–כלל” mainly when tying back to the section title.

### 4.5 “הכלל הנבחן” must be concrete

The tested collective is never an abstract “collective.” It must be specified, for example:

* government;
* coalition;
* state;
* Netanyahu;
* leadership;
* public;
* national camp;
* media;
* public discourse;
* security establishment;
* IDF;
* Israeli society.

Do not write broad statements such as “the collective abandons the individual” without specifying both the collective and the moral referent.

### 4.6 Attributed duty, not declared duty

Use **החובה המיוחסת לו**, not **החובה המוצהרת**. The duty is attributed to the collective by the article and reconstructed by our interpretation; it is not necessarily declared by the collective itself.

### 4.7 Analytic voice

The analysis is ours. Do not adopt the verdict of the articles as the researcher’s own claim.

Preferred formulations:

* המאמר מייחס לכלל כשל מוסרי בכך ש...
* במונחי המאמר, הכלל נכשל כאשר...
* המבחן שהמאמר בונה הוא...
* המאמר מציג את העסקה כ...

Avoid formulations that sound like the thesis endorses the article’s claim as fact:

* הכשל הוא הפקרה...
* הממשלה מפקירה...
* העסקה היא כניעה...
* הציבור נכשל מוסרית...

The point is to analyze how the article constructs a moral test, not to confirm the article’s verdict.

## 5. Writing mode for 1.1.1

The section should be written as qualitative findings/discussion prose, not as a technical mapping.

Preferred structure:

1. Conceptual opening paragraph.
2. Analytic movement through the main arenas/axes.
3. Substantial quote blocks only when they carry real weight.
4. Analytical reading of the quote.
5. Short synthesis paragraph.

Do not turn the section into:

* a table;
* a catalogue of all examples;
* a list of articles;
* 10–12 mini-readings by article;
* a purely technical mapping of object/collective/test.

The mapping tables are analytic infrastructure. The writing should be centripetal: one mechanism, with variations by type and by tested collective.

## 6. Current state of 1.1.1

### 6.1 Critical type

The Critical type is already mapped and should be written through three arenas:

1. **Hostages — חטופים**
2. **Soldiers — חיילים**
3. **Palestinians and innocent people — פלסטינים וחפים מפשע**

These are not degrees, stages, or a hierarchy. They are arenas in which the mechanism appears differently. Do not describe movement from hostages to soldiers to Palestinians as expansion or deepening unless a later analysis explicitly supports that claim.

### 6.2 Ultra-nationalist type

The Ultra-nationalist type is now also mapped. It should not be forced into the same three-arena structure as the Critical type.

The current accepted analytic structure is:

1. **Unified axis: Hostages and future victims — חטופים וקורבנות עתידיים**
2. **Soldiers and fallen soldiers — חיילים ונופלים**
3. **Concluding asymmetry: no Palestinian moral-referent axis**

This asymmetry is a finding. The Ultra-nationalist type does not mirror the Critical type’s Palestinian/civilian arena. Palestinians generally appear as enemy, threat, released prisoners/militants, Hamas, or justification for force — not as moral referents through whom the Israeli collective is morally tested.

## 7. Critical type — accepted content

### 7.1 Core argument

At the heart of the Critical type is a recurring interpretive move: a moral referent and a tested collective are brought alongside one another, and the collective is judged morally through what becomes visible in that relation.

Accepted Hebrew formulation:

> בלב הטיפוס הביקורתי עומד מהלך פרשני חוזר: מושא ייחוס מוסרי וכלל נבחן מובאים זה לצד זה, והכלל נשפט מוסרית לאור מה שמתגלה ביניהם.

The moral referent is not fixed, and the collective is not abstract or uniform. The collective can split into:

* government;
* coalition;
* public;
* media;
* IDF;
* Israeli society;
* public discourse.

The articles organize the discussion through categories such as “אנחנו”, hostages, soldiers, innocent people, and enemy. The point is not the categories themselves, but the moral test that emerges from the relation between the collective and the referent, as reconstructed by our interpretation.

### 7.2 Critical arena 1: Hostages

Primary articles used:

* **תכנית השלבים לחיסול החטופים**
* **אני מכחישה את טבח שמיני עצרת**
* **המסר של אילה חסון**
* supporting reference: **ארבע התצפיתניות**

Main analytic points:

* The government is judged by whether it treats the hostages’ lives as requiring rescue or as a price to be absorbed for political, military, or ideological goals.
* The public is judged not as a formal decision-maker, but through its failure or inability to force a different decision.
* The media is judged through the limits it sets on how hostage families may speak, grieve, rage, or demand accountability.
* Do not turn “abandonment” into the entire axis. It is central in some cases, but not a universal formula.

### 7.3 Critical arena 2: Soldiers

Soldiers are not always the moral referent. Sometimes they are the referent; sometimes they are agents of the collective.

Primary articles used:

* **תכנית השלבים לחיסול החטופים**
* **המסר של אילה חסון**
* **הביזוי של נשים פלסטיניות**
* supporting reference: **צה״ל מתעלל**

Two positions:

#### A. Soldiers as moral referents

They are those whom the state sends, risks, or sacrifices. The collective is judged by the risk it places on those sent to fight in its name.

Examples:

* soldiers whose only mission is to remain in the field until negotiations end;
* soldiers described as **ילדינו**;
* soldiers sacrificed **על מזבח חלומות משיחיים**.

#### B. Soldiers as agents of the collective

They are not the vulnerable referent but the extension of the collective. Their actions return responsibility to “us” as Israelis, to society, to the army, and to command.

Core quote:

> פשעיהם של חיילי צה״ל הם באחריותנו הישירה כישראלים.

### 7.4 Critical arena 3: Palestinians and innocent civilians

This is the most distinctive Critical arena and should not be compressed too aggressively.

Accepted phrasing:

> הפלסטינים — ובכללם נשים, ילדים בעלי שם וגיל, עצירים ואסירים, או אוכלוסיית עזה הרחבה — אינם נמנים עם הקולקטיב הלאומי הישראלי, אך הם נתונים לפעולתו, לשליטתו ולכוחו.

Refined phrasing:

> בחלק מן המופעים, אחריותו של הכלל כלפיהם אינה נגזרת מאוניברסליזם מופשט בלבד, אלא גם מעצם היותם נתונים לכוח ישראלי — צבאי, מדינתי או שיחי; היעדר שייכות לאומית לצד נתונות לכוח זה הוא שמטעין את המבחן.

Handle this carefully. Do not make every Palestinian-related instance identical or purely about occupation/physical control. Some are about discourse, category, recognition, emotional permission, or automatic moral classification.

Primary articles used:

* **הביזוי של נשים פלסטיניות**
* **7 באוקטובר לא היה שואה**
* **מבחינת עירית לינור**
* **האדונים מתרעמים** — used carefully, not overdeveloped
* **המורה התייפחה**
* **זו עסקה נפלאה, זו עסקה איומה**
* **אמנם מה שמתרחש בעזה אינו אושוויץ**

Main analytic moves:

* Palestinian women: the private body becomes a measure of Israeli power.
* Israeli feminist/women’s organizations: silence becomes part of the test.
* “Every Palestinian prisoner = terrorist / every IDF soldier = sacred”: Israeli discourse is judged through automatic moral classification.
* Named Palestinian child and grandfather vs. “עמלק”: a concrete human referent can be erased into a collective enemy category.
* Gaza’s children: the test concerns the limits of permitted empathy and grief.
* “גם בהם יש בני אדם”: not celebratory universalism, but critique of Israeli inability or refusal to see Gazans as human.
* Gaza and the hostages: in **אמנם מה שמתרחש בעזה אינו אושוויץ**, hostages and Palestinians enter the same moral balance, but they do not become the same referent.

### 7.5 Cross-arena distinction in the Critical type

Stated lightly, not imposed as a rigid schema:

* Hostages + soldiers: duty of the collective toward those counted within it.
* Palestinians and innocent people: responsibility arising from power over those outside national membership.

This distinction can appear in the summary but should not dominate the analytic reading.

## 8. Ultra-nationalist type — accepted content

### 8.1 General logic

The Ultra-nationalist type also judges the collective through its relation to moral referents, but the referents and the moral failure differ sharply from the Critical type.

The central question is not whether the collective abandons vulnerable individuals. It is whether the tested collective protects the threatened national collective from a deal that the articles present as producing future death, future hostages, weakened deterrence, released Palestinian prisoners/militants, Hamas survival, or renewed massacre.

This is not a generic security calculation. It is a moral hierarchy in which the threatened Israeli-Jewish collective, future victims, soldiers, fallen soldiers, deterrence, sacrifice, and national continuity are placed above immediate rescue through a deal represented as dangerous.

### 8.2 Unified axis: Hostages and future victims

Do not write “hostages” and “future victims” as two independent axes. They are two sides of one moral configuration.

Accepted formulation:

> החטופים הם הצד שמייצר את הלחץ המוסרי המיידי, ואילו הקורבנות העתידיים הם עמוד השדרה של המבחן האולטרה־לאומי — הם מגלמים את ביטחון וקיום הכלל בעתיד.

In this axis:

* The hostages are not erased as moral referents.
* They generate immediate moral pressure, often through pain, family pressure, media focus, the duty of rescue, or a campaign of compassion.
* The future victims are not merely an outcome of the deal. They are the moral referent through whom the threatened national collective is made present.
* The price Israel “pays” is not the Israeli hostages. It is mainly the release of Palestinian prisoners/militants and the future consequences attributed to that release: murder, future abductions, harm to deterrence, Hamas strengthening, renewed fighting, or another massacre.

### 8.3 Tested collective vs. threatened collective

This distinction is mandatory in the Ultra-nationalist analysis.

| Component | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **הכלל הנבחן** | The actor morally judged by the article: state, government, Netanyahu, leadership, public, national camp, media/discourse, security establishment. |
| **הקולקטיב המאויים / מושא הסכנה** | Those who are presented as likely to pay the future price: Israeli citizens, Jews, soldiers, future hostages, the next generation, residents of threatened areas, or “כולנו”. |

Do not write as if the state/government/public being judged is automatically identical with the threatened collective. The Ultra-nationalist article often judges one actor through its responsibility, loyalty, or failure toward a broader national collective under future threat.

### 8.4 The price of the deal

When the articles discuss “the price Israel will pay,” do not confuse Israeli hostages with Palestinian prisoners/militants released in the deal.

Analytically:

* Israeli hostages = source of immediate moral pressure and duty.
* Released Palestinian prisoners/militants = the price and the source of future threat in many articles.
* Future Israeli/Jewish victims = the threatened moral referent through whom the collective is judged.

### 8.5 Summary table for writing the Ultra-nationalist unified axis

Do not write from the detailed 11-row table article by article. Use it as a coding layer. The writing should be guided by this summary table:

| Tested collective | Type of moral failure attributed to it | Writing use |
|---|---|---|
| State / government | Sovereign-protective failure | The state is judged by whether it protects the threatened collective or transfers the future price of the deal onto it. |
| Netanyahu / leadership | Leadership-historical failure | Leadership is judged through memory of past deals/withdrawals, future blood, and the ability not to translate immediate duty into a deal presented as producing future victims. |
| Security establishment | Institutional-expert failure | The security establishment is judged by whether it turns future blood risk into a manageable technical problem and grants professional legitimacy to the deal. |
| Media / public discourse | Failure of moral legibility | Discourse is judged by whether future victims and those warning about them can appear as legitimate moral claims. |
| Public / national camp | Failure to publicly foreground future victims | The public/national camp is judged by whether it can place future victims before the public against a campaign of compassion and organized emotional framing. |

Use this table as the backbone for writing, not the detailed mapping table.

### 8.6 Detailed table status

The detailed unified table of **חטופים וקורבנות עתידיים** is useful as a coding/mapping layer only. Do not write the subsection as 11 mini-readings.

Required corrections to keep in the table and in writing:

* Use analytic attribution: “המאמר מייחס...”, “המאמר מציג...”, “במונחי המאמר...”.
* Do not adopt the article’s verdict as the thesis’s own claim.
* Keep the moral indictment, but tie it to the relation between tested collective and moral referent.
* Do not let the indictment become a general evaluation of political competence, leadership history, or mobilization capacity unrelated to the referent.
* Do not leave “קיום המדינה” as an abstract moral referent; translate it into the threatened Israeli-Jewish collective or concrete human groups.
* Keep “campaign of compassion,” “at any price,” and similar present-pressure language as evidence of the relation, not as the main argument of 1.1.1.

### 8.7 Ultra-nationalist soldiers and fallen soldiers

The second Ultra-nationalist axis is **soldiers / fallen soldiers**.

The moral referent is the human group: soldiers, fighters, fallen soldiers, IDF soldiers, or a concrete fighting camp. The referent is not sacrifice, blood, victory, meaning, or honor. Those are elements of the relation and the moral test.

Accepted formulation:

> החיילים והנופלים אינם רק מי שהכלל חייב להגן עליהם או לכבד אותם; הם נעשים הוכחה מוסרית לכך שהמלחמה חייבת להימשך עד ניצחון.

The tested collective is judged by its loyalty to military sacrifice, blood, heroism, and the meaning of death: whether it continues toward victory, or whether it wastes, desecrates, empties, or betrays the sacrifice through a deal, surrender, ceasefire, or moral code that prioritizes enemy lives over fighters’ lives.

Distinctive Ultra-nationalist vocabulary in this axis:

* sacrifice;
* heroism;
* blood;
* meaning;
* honor;
* humiliation;
* loyalty;
* revenge;
* decision/decisiveness;
* victory;
* prohibition on surrender.

Caution: this axis can easily slide into 1.1.2 because it often points toward “continue until victory.” In 1.1.1, use it only to show what moral test the soldiers/fallen generate for the collective. The binary demand belongs to 1.1.2.

### 8.8 No Palestinian moral-referent axis in the Ultra-nationalist type

In the Critical type, Palestinians and innocent people are a major arena of moral reference. In the Ultra-nationalist type, there is no equivalent axis.

This is not a missing category. It is a finding.

In Ultra-nationalist articles, Palestinians generally appear as:

* enemy;
* Hamas;
* terrorists;
* released prisoners/militants;
* threat;
* proof of the danger of weakness;
* justification for continued force.

They usually do not function as moral referents through whom the Israeli collective is judged. This asymmetry should appear as a concise concluding point, not as a full separate subsection unless later data require it.

## 9. Rejected or corrected framings

### 9.1 Structural rejections

| Rejected framing | Reason |
|---|---|
| Critical and Ultra-nationalist share the same individual-collective schema. | Too strong. They share an interpretive logic, not the same relation or objects. |
| Both types simply see hostages as the moral test of the state. | Too broad; this can also fit other nationalism types. |
| Critical puts the individual above the collective; Ultra-nationalist puts the collective above the individual. | Too simplistic; misses the different construction of moral failure. |
| Hostages → soldiers → Palestinians as degrees or hierarchy. | Rejected. These are arenas in the Critical type, not stages of expansion. |
| Writing by table row or article list. | Produces a catalogue rather than analysis. |
| Turning “abandonment” into the entire Critical axis. | Accurate in some cases, too broad as total frame. |
| Turning “many vs. few” into the entire Ultra-nationalist axis. | Useful heuristic, too thin analytically. |

### 9.2 Conceptual rejections

| Rejected concept as organizer | Reason |
|---|---|
| Moralization / מוסריזציה as the central frame | Present in background, but not the organizing concept of 1.1.1. |
| Boundary-work / עבודת גבולות as explicit frame | Relevant but not foregrounded; the section asks what the collective’s relation to referents reveals about its moral character. |
| Citizenship discourse as explicit theoretical frame | May be implicit in hostages/soldiers, but not named as a central frame. |
| “Positive moment” framing | Rejected; apparent recognition is usually read as critique/indictment of the collective. |
| Structural-position taxonomy | Too heavy; the configuration sense of relation is sufficient. |

### 9.3 Language rejections

| Rejected language | Decision |
|---|---|
| “חיים קונקרטיים” | Too general; do not use as organizing term. |
| “גופים פגיעים” | Sounds analytic but is empty for the reader. |
| “הפרט בר־הפקרה” | Abandonment is an action/relationship, not an essence of the referent. |
| “הכלל מפקיר” | Accurate only in some cases; do not generalize. |
| “ביטחון/הרתעה = הכלל” | Security and deterrence are not the collective; they are elements in the test or relation. |
| “מוצב מול” as dominant formula | Allowed, but vary it; do not let it replace specific analytic action. |
| “מחיקה” as blanket term for the Palestinian arena | Fits some cases, not the whole arena. |
| “קיום המדינה” as moral referent | Not allowed unless translated into the human collective it stands for in the article. |

## 10. Historical context of the data

The corpus centers on the period around the beginning of the second hostage deal between Israel and Hamas in early 2025.

During the period:

* the deal began;
* several rounds of hostage releases occurred;
* Israel and Hamas had already been in intense war for more than a year;
* in the Critical type, accusations regarding Gaza and genocide are part of the background;
* in the Ultra-nationalist type, past deals, released prisoners/militants, deterrence, Hamas survival, and future violence are central background references.

The deal is not merely a diplomatic event. It is the event that activates immediate moral judgment. This matters because Theme 1 concerns the present as a moment of moral decision, but 1.1.1 must still focus on the test itself rather than the full closing of the present.

## 11. Theoretical guidance

Stay in conversation with the literature review quietly. Do not over-perform theory in the findings text.

Relevant frames:

* cultural sociology: categories, meaning-making, symbolic classification;
* sociology of morality: responsibility, guilt, failure, obligation, recognition;
* political sociology: the collective is not abstract; it splits into government, public, media, military, society;
* citizenship: especially in hostages and soldiers, duties toward those abandoned, sent, risked, or silenced;
* nationalism: who counts as part of the national collective and how non-members or enemies appear;
* boundary-work: relevant, but not as an explicit label unless necessary;
* moralization: background only, not the organizing argument;
* moral conviction: mostly belongs to 1.1.2, where the test becomes a demand for immediate, non-compromising decision.

Important decision:

Do not make “moralization” the central concept of this subsection. The argument is not that issues become moral. The argument is that the collective is morally judged through the relation between itself and moral referents.
