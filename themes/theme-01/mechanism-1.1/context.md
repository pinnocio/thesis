## **Summary: Process and Current State of 1.1.1 — Critical Type**

### **Project Context**

This is the findings chapter of an MA thesis in sociology. The research examines how different nationalism types morally frame key ethical dilemmas of the Israel-Hamas war, using a corpus of ~180 online op-ed articles from the period around the second hostage deal (January 2025). The analytical framework is Feinstein's five-type nationalism typology, operationalized through reflexive thematic analysis and grounded theory. The five types are: critical, ultra-nationalist, ethno-religious, ethno-republican, ethnocentric.

The current work is on **Theme 1: Closing the Present as a Moral Decision Moment** — specifically on **Mechanism 1.1: Individual–Collective Relation as Moral Test of the Collective**, and within that on **Section 1.1.1: The individual–collective relation as a measure of the collective's moral standing**, critical type only.

---

## **Theme 1, Mechanism 1, Section 1.1.1 — Critical Type**

We are working on the findings chapter of an MA thesis. The current broader theme is:

**Theme 1: Closing the present as a moment of moral decision**  
Hebrew: **תמה 1: סגירת ההווה כרגע הכרעה מוסרי**

The theme examines a structural/interpretive resemblance between the **Critical** type and the **Ultra-nationalist** type. They do not agree substantively. Their political and moral conclusions are opposed. The resemblance is that both frame the present as a moment requiring an immediate, unequivocal moral decision, with little or no legitimate middle ground.

Within this theme, we are currently working on Mechanism 1:

**1.1 The relation between moral referent and collective as a moral test of the collective**  
Hebrew working title: **1.1 יחס פרט–כלל כמבחן מוסרי של הכלל**

The immediate subsection is:

**1.1.1 יחס פרט–כלל כמדד למבחן המוסרי של הכלל — הטיפוס הביקורתי**

At this stage, we are working only on the **Critical type** because we have enough mapped data for it. The Ultra-nationalist part will come later. Only after both parts of 1.1.1 are drafted should we move to 1.1.2.

## **The role of 1.1.1**

1.1.1 should show **what the moral test is**.

It should show how the morality of a collective is judged through the relation between that collective and a moral referent.

It should not yet fully develop the issue of:

* no legitimate middle ground;  
* urgency;  
* present decision;  
* deal versus continued war;  
* moral conviction;  
* binary closure of the present.

Those belong mainly to **1.1.2**.

---

### **What 1.1.1 Does, and How It Fits the Larger Structure**

**1.1 overall:** The convergence between the critical and ultra-nationalist types is not agreement, but a shared interpretive frame in which the present moment is closed as a site of moral decision with no legitimate middle ground. The relation between individual and collective is the first mechanism showing this.

**1.1.1 vs. 1.1.2:**

* 1.1.1 answers: *What is the moral test?* — The collective is judged by how it and the moral reference object stand in relation to each other.  
* 1.1.2 answers: *What does this test do to the present?* — It converges into a demand for immediate, binary decision.

1.1.1 must not develop urgency, present-moment closure, or binary demand — those belong to 1.1.2. A single transitional sentence at the end is sufficient.

**The comparison between critical and ultra-nationalist** is at the level of *interpretive logic*, not at the level of identical objects or axes. Both types judge the collective's morality through its relation to a moral reference object. They do not share the same objects, definitions, or positions — only the logic. The asymmetry in objects across types is not a problem; it is the finding.

### **Core Terminology and Decisions**

**"טיפוס" not "דפוס"** — Feinstein's typology uses types (טיפוסים), not patterns. This applies throughout.

**"מושא ייחוס מוסרי"** (moral reference object) — the preferred term in the analytic sections. "פרט" (individual) is retained in the section title and occasionally in body text to tie back to it, but is not the leading analytic term.

**"יחס פרט–כלל" = relation, not attitude:**

* The noun "יחס" when used as the central analytic term means *relation/configuration* — how individual and collective stand together, what is revealed about the collective when the two are held in view.  
* Treatment verbs (מפקיר, מסכן, מכיר, משתיק) are welcome and flow freely — they are the *evidence* disclosing the configuration.  
* The word "יחס" must not slide into meaning *way of treating* (attitude). It is reserved for the configuration sense.  
* Both senses coexist legitimately in the text; only the noun "יחס" as an analytic term must carry the configuration sense.

**"הכלל" (the collective) is always concrete** — government, coalition, public, media, IDF, Israeli society — never abstract.

**"החובה המיוחסת לו"** — not "החובה המוצהרת" (declared) — the duty is attributed to the collective by the article, not declared by the collective itself. This is an RTA/faithfulness-to-data requirement.

**"כוחו" not "שליטתו"** — "power" is used rather than "control/domination" in the Palestinian zone, because the power is sometimes military, sometimes state, sometimes discursive. "שליטה" is too narrow and physical.

**In the summary and body:** "יחס פרט–כלל" as a body-text explanatory term is replaced with "היחס שבין מושא הייחוס לבין הכלל" — except where it serves to tie back to the section title.

### **What Was Explicitly Rejected**

**Structural:**

* "Three deepening levels" or hierarchy across zones — rejected. The zones are three *areas* where the mechanism operates differently, not a deepening or progression. The presentational order (hostages → soldiers → Palestinians) is a choice, not an analytic claim.  
* Separate subsections per mechanism type — rejected. The section is comparative by design.

**Conceptual:**

* Moralization (מוסריזציה, Rozin) as an organizing concept — rejected as central frame. Present in background but not named or foregrounded.  
* Boundary-work (עבודת גבולות, Lamont) language — deliberately kept out. The Palestinian zone is framed as a moral test of the collective, not as a question of who belongs to the community. The distinction is: *what the collective's relation to outsiders reveals about its moral character* (stays), vs. *where the line falls between inside and outside* (rejected — belongs to a different theme).  
* Citizenship discourse (Shafir & Peled) as explicit frame — present in the hostages/soldiers zones implicitly (duty of state toward those it sends), but not named. Not extended into the critical type in ways that blur distinctions with the ethno-republican type.  
* "Positive moment" framing — rejected for both the Aila Hassan instance and the Gaza-as-human-beings instance. Both are reframed as critique/indictment of the collective.

**Language:**

* "מוצב מול" (positioned against) as dominant formula — reduced; varied language preferred.  
* "מחיקה" (erasure/deletion) as an overarching frame for the Palestinian zone — demoted to one instance within a broader spectrum (erasure, dehumanization, silencing, double standard, willful blindness, absent empathy).  
* Structural-position taxonomy (inside/outside, mobile, subjected-outsider) — rejected as too heavy; the basic configuration sense of יחס is sufficient.

## **2. Key conceptual decision: "פרט" is not a narrow individual**

The term **פרט** in the section title should not be treated as a narrow biographical individual. We need to find a better term than **פרט**, since the definition is different; **פרט** is an older concept that needs development in terms of definition.

The working concept (needs work on title) is:

**מושא ייחוס מוסרי** — a moral referent: a person or a distinguishable human group through whom the morality of a broader collective is examined.

The moral referent may include:

* hostages;  
* soldiers;  
* families of hostages;  
* Palestinian women;  
* Palestinian children;  
* children in Gaza;  
* Palestinian prisoners/detainees;  
* civilians;  
* innocent people;  
* people in Gaza.

But not every collective is automatically a moral referent.

Important cautions — the following aren't "**פרט**", but there might be exceptions since **פרט** is still a working term:

* A state is not.  
* A whole public is usually not.  
* A whole society is not usually not.  
* A feeling is not!  
* A campaign is not.  
* "The existence of the state" is not.  
* **Soldiers are not always the moral referent**; sometimes they are agents of the collective.  
* Palestinians / Gazans / civilians / children / prisoners can function as moral referents, but each case must be checked in context.

## **6. The accepted current argument**

The accepted draft begins from this core idea:

**At the heart of the Critical type is a recurring interpretive move: a moral referent and a collective are brought alongside one another, and the collective is morally judged through what becomes visible in that relation.**

Hebrew phrasing in the accepted draft:

**"בלב הטיפוס הביקורתי עומד מהלך פרשני חוזר: מושא ייחוס מוסרי וכלל נבחן מובאים זה לצד זה, והכלל נשפט מוסרית לאור מה שמתגלה ביניהם."**

The accepted draft further states that the moral referent is not fixed, and the collective is not abstract or uniform. The collective splits into distinct entities:

* government;  
* coalition;  
* public;  
* media;  
* IDF;  
* Israeli society as a whole.

The articles organize discussion through categories such as:

* "אנחנו"  
* hostages  
* soldiers  
* innocent people  
* enemy

But the point is not merely the categories themselves. הנקודה היא המבחן המוסרי שעולה מהיחס שבין הקולקטיב לבין המושא, כפי שעולה מהמאמרים - באופן פרשני שלנו, לא באופן ישיר ומוצהר של הכותבים. זה החלק האנליטי בעבודה.

## **4. Key structural decision: not "degrees", but arenas**

We rejected the idea that hostages → soldiers → Palestinians are "degrees" or a hierarchy.

They are not stages of deepening or expansion.

They are **three arenas**:

1. Hostages — **חטופים**  
2. Soldiers — **חיילים**  
3. Palestinians and innocent people — **פלסטינים וחפים מפשע**

Each arena reveals a different aspect of the collective being judged.

Current accepted formulation (work-in-progress):

* Through hostages, the collective is tested by its duty to save those who belong to it, and by the possibility of abandoning or silencing those who demand rescue.  
* Through soldiers, the collective is tested both by its duty toward those it sends to fight and by its responsibility for those who act in its name.  
* Through Palestinians and innocent people, the collective is tested by how it exercises power over those who are not part of the Israeli national collective but are subject to Israeli military, state, or discursive power.  
* It is also tested (the collective) by what's allowed and not allowed in discourse and media, by how it portrays the suffering of others (or disregards it), and perhaps more that we've yet to find — that's why it's a work-in-progress.

## **7. Arena 1: Hostages**

Primary articles used:

* **"תכנית השלבים לחיסול החטופים"**  
* **"אני מכחישה את טבח שמיני עצרת"**  
* **"המסר של אילה חסון"**  
* supporting reference: **"ארבע התצפיתניות"**

Main analytical points:

* The government is judged through whether it treats the hostages' lives as requiring rescue or as a price to be absorbed for political/ideological goals. And by allocation of time and attention: for example, a committee that finds time for symbolic legislation but not for hostage parents.  
* The public is also judged: not as formal decision-maker, but through its failure or inability to force a different decision.  
* The media is judged through the limits it sets on how hostage families may speak, grieve, rage, or demand accountability. And through that the Israeli society is also measured in terms of allowed or disallowed discourse, and how "the other" is portrayed and how much attention it is getting (the other is usually the Palestinians but also western media and countries).

* turning "abandonment" into the entire axis of the section;

## **8. Arena 2: Soldiers**

Soldiers are not always the moral referent. Sometimes they are the referent; sometimes they are agents of the collective.

Primary articles used:

* **"תכנית השלבים לחיסול החטופים"**  
* **"המסר של אילה חסון"**  
* **"הביזוי של נשים פלסטיניות"**  
* supporting reference: **"צה״ל מתעלל"**

Two positions of soldiers:

### **A. Soldiers as moral referents**

They are those whom the state sends, risks, or sacrifices.  
The collective is judged by the risk it places on those sent to fight in its name.

Examples:

* soldiers whose only mission is to remain in the field until negotiations end;  
* soldiers described as **"ילדינו"**;  
* soldiers sacrificed **"על מזבח חלומות משיחיים"**.

### **B. Soldiers as agents of the collective**

They are not the vulnerable referent but the extension of the collective.  
Their actions return responsibility to "us" as Israelis, to society, to the army, and to command.

Core quote:  
**"פשעיהם של חיילי צה״ל הם באחריותנו הישירה כישראלים."**

## **9. Arena 3: Palestinians and innocent civilians**

This is the most distinctive arena of the Critical type and should not be compressed too aggressively.

Accepted phrasing:  
**"הפלסטינים — ובכללם נשים, ילדים בעלי שם וגיל, עצירים ואסירים, או אוכלוסיית עזה הרחבה — אינם נמנים עם הקולקטיב הלאומי הישראלי, אך הם נתונים לפעולתו, לשליטתו ולכוחו."**

A later refinement was added:  
**"בחלק מן המופעים, אחריותו של הכלל כלפיהם אינה נגזרת מאוניברסליזם מופשט בלבד, אלא גם מעצם היותם נתונים לכוח ישראלי — צבאי, מדינתי או שיחי; היעדר שייכות לאומית לצד נתונות לכוח זה הוא שמטעין את המבחן."**

This is important but should be handled carefully. It is strong. It should not become a claim that every Palestinian-related instance is identical or purely about occupation/physical control. Some are about discourse, category, recognition, and emotional permission.

Primary articles used:

* **"הביזוי של נשים פלסטיניות"**  
* **"7 באוקטובר לא היה שואה"**  
* **"מבחינת עירית לינור"**  
* **"האדונים מתרעמים"** — used carefully, not overdeveloped  
* **"המורה התייפחה"**  
* **"זו עסקה נפלאה, זו עסקה איומה"**  
* **"אמנם מה שמתרחש בעזה אינו אושוויץ"**

Main analytical moves:

* Palestinian women: the private body becomes a measure of Israeli power.  
* Israeli feminist/women's organizations: silence becomes part of the test.  
* "Every Palestinian prisoner = terrorist / every IDF soldier = sacred": the Israeli discourse is judged through automatic moral classification.  
* Named Palestinian child and grandfather vs "עמלק": the text shows how a concrete human referent can be erased into a collective category.  
* Gaza's children: the test concerns the limits of permitted empathy and grief.  
* "גם בהם יש בני אדם": not celebratory universalism, but critique of Israeli inability or refusal to see Gazans as human.  
* Gaza and the hostages: in "אמנם מה שמתרחש בעזה אינו אושוויץ", hostages and Palestinians enter the same moral balance, but they do not become the same referent.

## **11. Theoretical guidance for continuation**

The analysis should stay in conversation with the literature review, but quietly.

Relevant theoretical frames:

* Cultural sociology: categories, meaning-making, symbolic classification.  
* Sociology of morality: responsibility, guilt, failure, obligation, recognition.  
* Political sociology: the collective is not abstract; it splits into government, public, media, military, society.  
* Citizenship: especially in hostages and soldiers — duties of state/society toward those abandoned, sent, risked, or silenced.  
* Nationalism: especially in Palestinians — the moral boundary between those who belong to the national collective and those outside it.  
* Boundary-work: relevant, but not as an explicit label unless necessary.  
* Moralization: part of the background, but not the organizing argument of 1.1.1.  
* Moral conviction: mostly belongs to 1.1.2, where the moral test becomes a demand for immediate and non-compromising decision.

Important decision:  
Do not make "moralization" the central concept of this subsection. The argument is not "issues become moral." The argument is that the collective is morally judged through the relation between itself and moral referents.

---

## **12. Style rules that emerged**

The user strongly preferred:

* Hebrew academic prose, but not generic or pompous.  
* Clear, flowing writing.  
* Findings/discussion style, not a technical table or code-like mapping.  
* Quote blocks when they carry real weight.  
* Fewer short quote fragments; use substantial excerpts when possible.  
* Lighter examples can be integrated into prose.  
* Do not over-explain what the argument is not.  
* Do not write defensive sentences aimed at a hypothetical confused reader.  
* Do not overuse "פרט–כלל" in the body. It can remain in the section title, but body text should use the more precise language where possible.  
* Do not overuse "הצבה" or "מוצב מול" mechanically. It is allowed because op-eds use rhetorical operations, but it should not replace the specific action: ranking, silencing, categorizing, particularizing, transferring responsibility, exposing blindness, etc.  
* Preserve elegant phrasing when it works. The user specifically liked language such as:  
  **"מערך שני נבנה סביב החיילים, אך כאן מיקומם משתנה בי
ן מופעים שונים. לעיתים החיילים מוצבים מול הממשלה כמי שנשלחים, מסוכנים או מוקרבים בשמה."**

The preferred writing mode is:

* measured;  
* conservative;  
* not over-rewritten;  
* analytically clear;  
* not overly technical;  
* not inflated;  
* not trying too hard to "perform theory."

## **13. Things explicitly rejected or corrected**

Rejected or corrected:

* Treating "פרט" as only a biographical individual.  
* Treating "יחס" as attitude/treatment rather than relation.  
* Claiming a full symmetry between Critical and Ultra-nationalist types.  
* Claiming that both types speak about the same referents, same collective, or same moral action.  
* Building a hierarchy of "degrees" from hostages to soldiers to Palestinians.  
* Overusing "moralization" as an organizing concept.  
* Turning the section into a list of examples.  
* Making the Palestinian section too short; it carries a distinctive contribution.  
* Making the section too technical.  
* Adding theoretical labels too explicitly.  
* Using "מחיקה" as a blanket term for the whole Critical pattern. It fits some Palestinian-related cases, especially "עמלק", but not the whole section.  
* Writing broad statements such as "the collective abandons the individual" without specifying which collective and which moral referent.
